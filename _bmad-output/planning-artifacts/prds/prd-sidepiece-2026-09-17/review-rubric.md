# PRD Quality Review — Sidepiece (prd-sidepiece-2026-09-17)

## Overall verdict

This is a strong PRD for its shape and stakes: a real thesis (page-declared identity, so resolution is exact rather than inferred), FRs that mostly carry numeric or enumerable consequences, non-goals that do actual work, and a brownfield reconciliation (§10) that I spot-checked against the live `SIDE` board and found accurate ticket-for-ticket. Calibration is right — no personas, no stakeholder theater, three one-line UJs, operational success metrics — and the `[ASSUMPTION]` roundtrip is clean.

The risk is concentrated in three places. FR-5 requires behaviour that §5's own panel-lifetime rule makes impossible and no other requirement funds. §8.1 commits MVP to FR-5/FR-6/FR-7 while §12 Q1 and Q2 say their enabling mechanisms are unestablished — the contingency is visible only if you read to §12. And the one prerequisite that makes the whole product non-inert — something emitting the `pjid` into served pages — is absent from §6's dependency table and parked as a "sequencing risk."

Fix those three and this is green-light-to-build. The remaining findings are sharpening, not repair.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions read as decisions. §4.2 does not "consider" streaming versus dispatch; it splits them, states why ("Conflating these is how the panel ends up appearing to hang while an agent does twenty minutes of work"), and addendum §A.4 names what both rejected alternatives cost. §5's trust-boundary bullet decides no auth, says it is "deliberate and correct for a single-operator tool," and gives a revisit condition rather than hedging. The origin-reach/permission-scope bullet is the best paragraph in the document: a genuine tradeoff, resolved, with a stated expiry ("If §7's portability non-goal is ever revisited, this decision is the first thing that breaks"). §10's per-epic verdicts are blunt — "Rewrite," "Defer and rewrite," "Split" — not softened into "revisit."

Open Questions are actually open. Q1 is flagged **Blocks architecture** with no answer smuggled into the next sentence. Q7 is honest that documentation cannot settle it ("Verify empirically; do not resolve from documentation"). Q5 is a defect found in a neighbouring system and logged rather than silently patched — I verified it: `event-schemas.md:49` still reads `slug` from `project_slug`, and `.project.json` now carries `project_id`. That is a real catch.

The one gap is that the blocking questions never propagate forward into the commitment section. §8.1 lists FR-5 flat alongside FR-9 as if both were equally settled.

### Findings

- **high** MVP scope commits to FRs whose enabling mechanism is unresolved (§8.1, §12 Q1–Q2) — §12 Q1 states flatly that "it is not established that the gateway provides" a synchronous streaming channel and marks itself **"Blocks architecture."** Q2 asks whether a correlation id even survives into the outcome event and whether "Candystore index[es] it" — FR-7's entire premise. Yet §8.1 lists "Streamed Exchange with the PM (FR-5)" and "Dispatched Command with acknowledgement and outcome (FR-6, FR-7)" with no contingency marker, no fallback trigger, and no decision point. `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` reading §8 alone will generate committed stories for three FRs, one third of the MVP, whose transport may not exist. Relatedly, FR-6 requires that "The subject conforms to the five-token contract and is validated before publish" but never names the concrete command type Sidepiece emits — and addendum §B.1 warns "a well-formed 4-token type whose action is not in the allowlist is refused," so an unnamed type is a third unverified prerequisite. *Fix:* mark FR-5/FR-6/FR-7 in §8.1 as contingent, name the fallback inline (Q1 already has one — CLI-passthrough, the old D3, which still exists on the board as ticket D3), name the intended command type (or add an Open Question asking which allowlisted type covers "assign work to a PM"), and state the gate: resolve Q1/Q2 against `bb contract` before the chat epic is written.

## Substance over theater — strong

Nothing here reads as furniture. §1 Vision cannot be swapped into another PRD — it names the mechanism, the specific services, and an explicit disclaimer of pane-level value ("The value is not any single pane"). §2 skips personas entirely in favour of four JTBD lines, which is the right call and saves two pages of fiction. §5's NFRs are the opposite of boilerplate: every bullet carries either a number, a named Chrome failure mode, or a rejected alternative. "Cost of being wrong" converts a philosophical stance into a testable rule ("the Bridge rejects a mutation whose pjid does not match the Board it targets").

Addendum §D.1 is the right way to handle prior art on a solo tool: four entries, explicitly framed as instructive rather than competitive ("competitive positioning is not a concern for a single-operator tool"), with Vercel Toolbar as premise validation and Sourcegraph as the cautionary tale about URL→repo mapping. No innovation theater — the claimed novelty (declaration over inference) is actually the differentiator and is argued, not asserted.

§13's "Verified, no longer assumptions" subsection is a small thing that does disproportionate work: it shows the research pass actually changed the document rather than being appended to it.

### Findings

- **low** SM-1 is stated as a target but nothing in the FRs makes it observable (§11) — "most new tickets on actively-browsed Projects originate in Sidepiece. Validates FR-9, FR-10." A ticket created through the Bridge and one created through `px` hit the same Plane API with the same credential and are indistinguishable afterwards. FR-10's consequences do not require any origin marker, so the metric cannot be evaluated even in principle. Same for SM-C1 ("Panel open time") — no requirement emits it. *Fix:* one consequence on FR-10 — the created Ticket carries a `source:sidepiece` label (or equivalent) — makes SM-1 checkable with a single `px` query. Drop SM-C1 or accept it as a felt signal rather than a measure, and say which.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

There is a thesis and it is load-bearing: resolution is exact because the page declares itself, therefore origin is irrelevant, therefore the localhost-versus-Traefik question from the June brief dissolves instead of being answered. Feature order follows from it — §4.1 is named "The spine — everything else is dead without it," and §7's "Not a URL matcher" defends the thesis against the most likely erosion ("A page that does not declare a pjid is not a Project, full stop"). MVP scope kind is coherent: problem-solving, the noticing→acting loop, with everything else specified-not-dropped in §9. SM-C2 is a genuinely good counter-metric because it is aimed at this project's actual historical failure ("This died once already at 44 tickets and zero code") — verified, the board does hold exactly 44 tickets, all backlog.

The hole is that the thesis has a precondition the PRD does not own. Declaration-based resolution only works if pages declare. Addendum §A.1 concedes this in one clause — "The cost is a bootstrapping burden — every Project must emit the declaration before it is visible" — and §12 Q4 admits "v1 is useless until some number of Projects actually declare one," then classifies it as "Sequencing risk, not a design risk" and moves on. That classification is defensible for the *design*, but it leaves the PRD's own success condition outside its scope, outside its dependency table, and unassigned.

### Findings

- **high** The pjid emission prerequisite is missing from §6's dependency table and has no entry condition (§6, §12 Q4) — §6 opens "Each dependency is listed with what breaks when it's gone" and lists six rows. The one dependency whose absence makes *every* FR untestable and the product inert is not among them. §12 Q4 leaves the mechanism genuinely undecided ("Is that a pjangler recipe, a per-project template change, or manual?") — but the mechanism is more settled than the PRD assumes: `pj recipe list|describe|run` exists today, as do `pj audit` ("Deterministic parity audit against 33god project standard") and `pj migrate` ("Idempotent migration recipe for a parity rule"), which is exactly the shape of "every registered Project must emit a meta tag." *Fix:* add a §6 row — *pjid declaration in served HTML | FR-1 detection | Total: no Project resolves; every FR is untestable* — and convert Q4 into a stated v1 entry condition (e.g. "v1 is not demoable until ≥3 Projects declare; tracked as a pjangler parity rule, not a Sidepiece story"). Name `pj migrate`/`pj recipe` as the leading candidate so Q4 is a choice rather than an open field.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The FR bodies are mostly excellent and unusually disciplined for a solo-tool PRD. Numbers where numbers belong (500ms detection, 1s p95 resolution, 2s p95 board read, 2s first token). Enumerations instead of adjectives — FR-3 names four states rather than saying "handles errors gracefully." Negative consequences that close the usual escape hatches: "No failure state renders as an empty or loading panel," "A stream that dies mid-response is reported as failed, not left indefinitely pending," "shown as unknown, not as success," "On failure, the entered text is preserved." FR-12's `curl` requirement is stated as "a hard requirement, not a convenience" with the reason attached. I found no "user-friendly," no "reasonable performance," no "handles X gracefully."

Three holes, though, and the first is a direct self-contradiction rather than a vagueness.

FR-5 requires that "Closing and reopening the panel mid-stream does not lose the turn." §5 Panel lifetime states that all long-lived connections "live in the panel document" and that the panel document "does not survive the panel closing." Addendum §C is sharper still: SSE "has no equivalent documented exemption" from service-worker idle death, so the stream cannot be parked there either. Closing the panel therefore kills the connection, and no requirement anywhere makes anything else hold the in-flight turn — §4.4's FRs cover health, lifecycle, and credentials, not buffering; §4.2's Out of Scope does not exclude it. The requirement is real, is in MVP scope, and is funded by nothing.

Second, the PRD has no refresh path at all. FR-2 caches resolution "per pjid for the panel session" with no invalidation rule; FR-9 fetches "fresh on Project resolution." Since resolution can be served from cache, "on Project resolution" is undefined for a return visit. The consequence bites precisely where the product is supposed to shine: FR-6 dispatches work to the PM, the PM moves tickets on the Board, and the ticket pane has no stated way to notice. Same for FR-4 — provision an agent using the command FR-4 names, and the cached Project Record still reports no PM.

Third, FR-3 claims exhaustiveness it does not deliver, and FR-11 contradicts it.

### Findings

- **high** FR-5's mid-stream survival requirement contradicts §5 and is funded by no other requirement (§4.2 FR-5, §5 Panel lifetime) — FR-5 requires "Closing and reopening the panel mid-stream does not lose the turn," while §5 states the panel document "does not survive the panel closing" and that outcomes arriving while the panel is shut "are reconciled on next open rather than streamed to a listener that isn't there." Nothing in §4.4 requires the Bridge to buffer or replay an in-flight Streamed Exchange, and §4.2's Out of Scope does not exclude it. An engineer implementing FR-5 literally will discover the requirement is unsatisfiable within the stated architecture. *Fix:* pick one. Either add an FR-11/FR-12-adjacent consequence — "the Bridge retains an in-flight Streamed Exchange addressable by turn id and supports resumption from last delivered token" — or rewrite FR-5's consequence to match §5: "a turn interrupted by panel close is reconciled on next open as completed or failed, never left pending." The second is cheaper and consistent with FR-7's reconciliation posture.
- **medium** No cache invalidation and no refresh path anywhere (§4.1 FR-2, §4.3 FR-9, §4.1 FR-4) — FR-2 says "Resolution results are cached per pjid for the panel session; returning to an already-resolved tab does not re-hit the Registry," with no TTL, no invalidation trigger, and no manual override. FR-9's "fetched fresh on Project resolution" is therefore undefined on a cache hit. The product's own loop breaks the invariant: dispatch work via FR-6, the PM mutates the Board, and the pane cannot show it; run FR-4's named provisioning command and the Cockpit still reports no PM. *Fix:* add an FR-2 consequence giving the cache an explicit invalidation rule (a refresh control, invalidation on observed dispatch outcome, or a TTL — state which), and an FR-9 consequence for an explicit user-initiated refetch that does not require navigating away.
- **medium** FR-3 claims exhaustive state coverage but FR-11 and §6 introduce states outside its four (§4.1 FR-3, §4.4 FR-11) — FR-3 asserts "Every way resolution can fail is distinguishable in the UI" and then enumerates exactly four: "no pjid declared; pjid declared but unknown to the Registry; Bridge unreachable; Registry readable but the Project's clone path is missing on disk." FR-11 then requires the Cockpit to distinguish "Bridge reachable, unreachable, and reachable-but-unhealthy," and §6 gives per-dependency failure semantics (Registry down is "Total," Plane down is "Tickets pane only"). Reachable-but-unhealthy-because-Registry-is-failing is a resolution failure and is not one of FR-3's four. Downstream will build two different state machines from the same PRD. *Fix:* make FR-3 the single authoritative state enumeration — fold in reachable-but-unhealthy (at minimum split by which dependency), and have FR-11 reference FR-3's enumeration rather than restating its own.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and mostly load-bearing. §7's six non-goals each close a door someone would otherwise walk through, and two of them ("Not a URL matcher," "Not a replacement for the terminal") defend the thesis rather than just trimming scope. §8.2 is not a dumping ground — its preamble states the reason for §9's existence ("so `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` has a shape to work from when it lands"), and §9 genuinely specifies each deferred item rather than naming it, including the element picker's known failure modes and the `captureVisibleTab` viewport constraint.

`[ASSUMPTION]` tags sit on real inferences, not safe ones: §2.2's permanence assumption explicitly names its own cost ("the design may take shortcuts that are expensive to undo"), and FR-6's biases the classifier with a stated asymmetry. The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts are both at genuine tensions — the Tauri bullet proposes deleting rather than carrying a stale item, and §9's agent-session note argues against its own deferral ("it is the only pane that prevents an actual collision. If parallel-agent work increases before v1 ships, promote it"). That is the opposite of a safe checkpoint.

Open-items density — seven Open Questions, six indexed assumptions, two PM notes — is appropriate for the stakes and would be low even for a higher-stakes document, given that four of the seven are marked non-blocking.

### Findings

- **medium** §12 Q3 omits the pjangler surface that already exists and is the one that fits the latency budget (§12 Q3) — the question offers two options: "`pj info <pjid>` shelling out, or a library/MCP surface? Shelling out is simplest and slowest." There is a third: `pj info --help` shows `--registry <location>  Registry service URL or fixture path (default: http://localhost:8764)`, and that port is currently listening on this workstation. The Registry is already an HTTP service; the Bridge can query it directly with no Node process spawn. This is not academic — FR-2 budgets "within 1s at the 95th percentile" and a cold Node CLI start consumes a meaningful fraction of that. *Fix:* add the registry service as the third option in Q3 and note it as the leading candidate on latency grounds, with the CLI retained as the fallback when the service is not running (which FR-11 must then report as a distinct unhealthy dependency).

## Downstream usability — strong

This PRD is chain-top by its own declaration (§0: "the contract between the Sidepiece brain dump and everything downstream"), so this dimension matters, and it holds up. The Glossary is present, fixes fourteen terms, and states the rule ("Introducing a synonym anywhere is a discipline violation"). Spot-checking the domain nouns across §4, §5, §6, §9 and §10: Project, Project Record, Board, Ticket, Cockpit, Bridge, Agent, PM, Streamed Exchange and Dispatched Command are used verbatim and capitalized consistently throughout, including in the §6 dependency table and the §10 verdict table. That is better discipline than most PRDs manage.

IDs are contiguous and unique: FR-1 through FR-13 with no gaps or duplicates, UJ-1..3, SM-1..3 plus SM-C1/SM-C2. Every cross-reference I followed resolves — Glossary "See §4.4" → the Bridge feature; FR-4 "see §9" → one-click provisioning; §4.4 Out of Scope "see §5" → the trust-boundary bullet; §5 "See §12 Q7" → the PNA question; §10's FR citations all land on real FRs. Each feature block names which UJs it realizes, and §11 maps each SM back to FRs, so traceability runs both directions.

§10 is the section that earns this verdict. I pulled the live `SIDE` board: 44 issues, EPIC A–K, created 2026-06-23, and the specifics check out — B3 is literally `B3 — GET /resolve?url= URL→repo resolver`, B2 is `holocene fleet client + project index`, D3 is `CLI-passthrough fallback`, K1 is `Secrets & config via op/.env` and K2 is `Bridge lifecycle (systemd --user)`, matching the FR-13/FR-12 split the table claims. A brownfield reconciliation whose existing-code references survive verification is rare enough to call out.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape is right and was chosen deliberately rather than defaulted into. This is a single-operator internal tool, and the PRD is a capability spec: thirteen FRs grouped under four features, each with testable consequences, plus a cross-cutting NFR section. §2.3's three one-line UJs are the correct density — they exist to anchor the feature blocks' "Realizes UJ-n" lines and to carry one edge case (UJ-3's bridge-down fallback), not to simulate multi-stakeholder discovery. Expanding them into narrative beats would be over-formalization, and the PRD says as much ("Single operator, single role — captured in the lighter shape per the template's scope dial"). Personas are absent, correctly. Success metrics are behavioural and operational rather than user-facing funnels, and §11 states why in one line ("Single-operator tool; the only honest measures are behavioural").

§0 is doing real shape work too: it declares the reader ("written for one reader who is also the only user"), states where length was spent ("on requirements that constrain implementation and almost none on justification"), and pushes transport and framework decisions into the addendum so the PRD stays a requirements document. The addendum then carries exactly what a PRD should not — rejected alternatives, Chrome MV3 mechanics, library comparisons — which is the correct split rather than a dodge.

The one arguable shape call is promoting the Bridge to a first-class feature (§4.4) when it is an implementation component. The PRD defends it explicitly ("since it is a process I have to keep alive, its health and lifecycle are product surface, not purely an implementation detail") and that reasoning holds: FR-11 and FR-12 are about the operator's experience of a daemon, which is genuinely product surface on a self-hosted tool. Correct call.

## Mechanical notes

- **§12 Q5 is right but understates the problem.** Verified: `.project.json` carries `"project_id": "sidepiece"`, and `_bmad/custom/workflows/ticket-lifecycle/data/event-schemas.md:49` still maps `slug` ← `project_slug`. Q5's framing ("One of the two is now wrong") misses that a Bridge reading `.project.json` and emitting events per that contract will now produce a `project_id` that means the slug where the schema expects the board UUID. Worth restating in Q5 so whoever resolves it fixes the collision rather than just repointing the reader.
- **§8.2 promises §9 coverage it does not deliver for one item.** "Everything below is specified in §9 rather than dropped" — but "Desktop shell (Tauri)" appears in §8.2 and not in §9. The accompanying `[NOTE FOR PM]` arguably supersedes the promise, but as written the claim is false for one of seven bullets. Either add a one-line §9 entry or reword to "everything below except the desktop shell."
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip is clean, with one drift.** All six inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (§2.2, FR-1, FR-4, FR-6, §5, §9) appear in §13, and all six §13 entries appear inline — no orphans in either direction. The index has indexed the requirement and dropped the assumption. Swap it.
- **"pane" is used throughout §5 and §6 but is not in the Glossary**, which defines Cockpit ("The Chrome side panel UI") but nothing for its sub-regions. §5's "Every pane fails independently" and §6's "Tickets pane only" / "Chat pane only" are the load-bearing failure-isolation contract, so the unit deserves a definition. - **Required sections for the agreed stakes are all present**, and the deliberate omissions recorded in `.decision-log.md` (stakeholders, approvals, rollout, ROI, compliance, data governance, monetization) are correctly absent. Two sections outside the standard menu — §9 Deferred Scope and §10 SIDE Board Reconciliation — both earn their place.
